§4  VERIFIED NO LOG POLICY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  Win's VPN does not collect or keep VPN activity logs. Win's VPN does not
     log browsing history, DNS query history, traffic contents, raw VPN traffic,
     or user destination activity.
4.2  The only limited service metadata stored is the minimum needed to operate
     the service: account status, payment/referral status, legal acceptance
     evidence, security/abuse-prevention records, privacy-preserving
     device-limit metadata for enforcing the three-device limit, and the
     free-access connection events described in §4.5.
4.3  Transient connection/session state is cleared when the VPN disconnects.
4.4  The service is designed and operated so that VPN activity data is not
     written to disk at a gateway or the hub. This describes how Win's VPN is
     built and run. It is not a claim that the operator is technically
     incapable of logging.
4.5  Free-access connection events. When a free-access session is verified or
     signed out, the service writes one event record containing the shared
     free-access account identity, a device bucket, a one-way hash of the
     source IP address, the device public-key fingerprint, platform and device
     model, the gateway selected, whether censorship-resistant transport was
     requested, a one-way hash of the user agent, and the time. The record
     shows that a connection was set up and which gateway served it, never what
     was done through it. It contains no browsing history, DNS query history,
     traffic contents, or destination activity. It is retained for 30 days and
     erased with the account. Paid accounts do not generate these records.
4.6  "Verified No Log Policy" is Win's VPN's name for the commitments in this
     section. As of this version they have not been independently audited or
     attested. The word "Verified" does not state or imply otherwise.

§5  DATA COLLECTED BY THE APP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  Random app-install identifier. After the user accepts the bundled Privacy
     Policy, the app generates a random app-install identifier on the device and
     transmits it to Win's VPN with account or free-access identity and device
     details (device model, platform, app version, locale, selected location,
     capability flags). The identifier is created locally on first run and is
     not derived from a device hardware identifier or an advertising identifier.
     On Android, Windows, and Linux it is kept in the app's private storage and
     reinstalling produces a new one; on Apple platforms it is kept in the
     device keychain and can survive a reinstall on the same device. It may be
     linked to the account for app functionality, account management, the
     three-device limit, fraud prevention, security, and abuse prevention. It is
     not used for advertising or tracking, sold, or shared for advertising. The
     hub protects the retained device-limit value; it is deleted or overwritten
     when the device is deactivated, replaced, or no longer needed.
5.2  Device cryptographic identity. The app generates a long-lived cryptographic
     key pair on first use of each account and derives a public key from it. The
     public key is transmitted to Win's VPN with every account verification and
     configuration renewal request, not only at activation, and is used as a
     persistent identifier for connection provisioning, the three-device limit,
     and abuse prevention. It is a long-lived linkable identifier: it stays the
     same across app relaunches and reconnects and therefore functions as a
     repeatable device marker, not a one-time token. It is not derived from
     hardware identifiers or advertising identifiers. The private key never
     leaves the device; only the derived public key is transmitted. The key pair
     is stored locally in the app's private storage or the platform keychain,
     and the corresponding account-side record is deleted or overwritten when
     the device is deactivated, replaced, or no longer needed.
5.3  Additional data categories. The app sends the device locale, the VPN
     location selected, and capability flags describing which features the build
     and platform support, so the service can return a working configuration.
     The legal acceptance record also stores the email address supplied for a
     paid or manual account, a one-way hash of the user agent, and the locale.
     Where payment is made through PayPal, the payer name and the PayPal payer,
     subscription, plan, and purchase references supplied by PayPal are stored
     with the account.
5.4  Coarse public-IP location and map. Only after the user asks to show the
     approximate-location map, and before the VPN connects, the app asks
     compiled first-party Win's VPN origins and gateways for an IP-derived map
     scene. Cloudflare's network-metadata endpoint
     (speed.cloudflare.com/meta) is the final fallback. Win's VPN and any
     fallback endpoint receive the pre-VPN connection IP and ordinary request
     metadata. On Apple platforms Apple Maps receives the requested map region
     and connection IP. On other platforms a first-party Win's VPN endpoint
     serves OpenStreetMap-derived tiles, so its upstream sees the Win's VPN
     server rather than the device. Win's VPN does not request device GPS for
     this feature or retain the returned IP, city, postal code, or map-view
     history. The most recent coarse coordinates may remain in the app's
     protected local settings on the device. They are not transmitted to
     Win's VPN. On Android, Windows, and Linux they are removed by clearing the
     app's data or uninstalling the app; on Apple platforms they are held in
     the device keychain and can survive a reinstall on the same device.
5.5  Live public-IP display. After the user accepts the bundled Privacy Policy,
     and while the app is disconnected, it may query the third-party service
     api.ipify.org to display the device's current public IP address next to the
     connection status indicator. That service receives the connection IP and
     ordinary request metadata; no account number, device identifier, or other
     app data is sent. The request is never made while a VPN tunnel is active.
5.6  Speed-test endpoints. When the user starts the in-app speed test, the app
     measures throughput by downloading from and uploading to third-party test
     servers: speed.cloudflare.com and cachefly.cachefly.net for every region,
     and, for nodes in the western United States,
     losangeles.ca.speedtest.frontier.com,
     lsanca-speedtest-ookla-02.st.charter.com, and speedtest.lax2.sonic.net.
     Each server receives the connection IP, ordinary request metadata, and the
     transferred measurement traffic; no account number, device identifier, or
     other app data is included. These requests occur only while the user runs
     the speed test.
5.7  Gateway recovery and third-party connections. So the app can still reach a
     working gateway when the usual DNS or CDN paths are censored, it requests a
     small gateway-recovery file hosted on GitHub at raw.githubusercontent.com.
     GitHub necessarily receives the connection IP and ordinary request metadata
     for that request. The file can only select among gateways already built
     into the signed app, and no account number, device identifier, or other
     user data is sent. This request is made only after the user accepts the
     bundled Privacy Policy.
5.8  Split-tunneling selections. Selected split-tunneling package identifiers
     stay in protected local settings and are passed locally to the
     operating-system VPN service; the full installed-app inventory is not
     uploaded. See §16.2 for the Android installed-app disclosure.
5.9  Website data. The public website may collect: email address when provided
     for paid/manual accounts or support, prepaid redemption codes submitted on
     the website, security vulnerability reports submitted through the disclosure
     form, support messages the user sends, and website analytics or
     ad-conversion events when consented to on the website. Website marketing
     tracking is separate from VPN tunnel traffic and does not track what users
     do through the VPN.
5.10 Diagnostics and crash information. The app contains no third-party crash
     or analytics SDK and does not upload crash reports. A distribution
     platform may provide aggregated crash or diagnostic information to the
     developer under its own controls. Support messages and diagnostic detail
     are processed only when the user or platform supplies them.

§6  ABUSE PREVENTION AND RATE LIMITING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  To throttle repeated failed attempts and protect accounts, gateways, and
     infrastructure, the service keeps short-lived request counters and
     security-event records keyed to a hashed form of the source IP address,
     device identifier, or account number rather than to the value itself.

     and device-limit enforcement. They are not used to profile a user, are not
     used for advertising, and never contain VPN traffic, browsing history, DNS
     query history, or destination activity.

§14  USER CHOICES AND RIGHTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
14.1 Withdrawing consent. Consent can be withdrawn at any time by tapping
     Deactivate Device (which ends the session on that device and frees its
     slot), by clearing the app's data, or by uninstalling the app. Each stops
     further collection from that device. Split-tunneling selections can be
     removed at any time in the app's settings. To withdraw consent for data the
     service already holds, or to object to its further processing, email
     privacy@winsvpn.org. Website analytics and advertising-conversion events
     are processed only where consent has been given on the website.
14.2 Access and correction. Requests for access to or correction of personal
     data are handled by email at privacy@winsvpn.org or support@winsvpn.org.
     Send the request from the email address associated with the account, or
     include the account number, so the request can be verified.
14.3 Deleting your account from inside the app. Account deletion is a
     self-service feature. Open the app on an activated device and choose
     Delete Account, shown directly below Deactivate Device on the main account
     screen. The app states the rule that applies, shows a plain warning that
     deletion is permanent, and requires a second explicit confirmation before
     anything is sent. No account is ever deleted on the strength of an account
     number alone: every step is authenticated with the individual device's own
     credential.
14.4 Multi-device confirmation. So a single lost or stolen device cannot destroy
     an account, deletion normally requires confirmation from two different
     active devices. The exact rule is derived from the number of devices
     actually active on the account at the moment requested:
       Three active devices: any two must confirm (a lost or broken device does
         not prevent deletion).
       Two active devices: both must confirm.
       One active device: that device confirms and the account enters a
         seven-day waiting period, after which the account and its data are
         erased automatically. Signing in on any device during those seven days
         and choosing Cancel Deletion stops it.
       No active devices: there is no device that can authorise the erase.
         Activate a device with the account number and setup key, then delete
         the account from it, or write to the addresses in §22.
     While a deletion is pending, the app shows how many confirmations have been
     received out of the number required, and any active device on the account
     can cancel it. Deactivating a device is not the same as deleting an
     account: deactivation removes only that device's record and frees its slot
     toward the three-device limit.
14.5 What deletion erases. When a deletion completes, the following are
     permanently deleted, not merely marked inactive or hidden: the account
     record (account number, any account email address, account status,
     expiration date, referral code, and the stored hash of the account setup
     key); every device record on the account (each device's stored WireGuard
     private key, public key, pre-shared key, assigned tunnel IP address, device
     model, and device credential); any Double VPN assignment for those devices;
     the account's legal-acceptance records; any free-access event records tied
     to the account; and any unclaimed prepaid-redemption fulfilment record or
     queued delivery email for the account. The corresponding VPN peers are also
     removed from the gateway servers, so the tunnel stops working rather than
     merely losing its database entry. Deletion is immediate and irreversible:
     any remaining paid time, lifetime access, tech-support access, or referral
     bonus is forfeited and is not refunded, and referral links shared by the
     user stop working.
14.6 What is retained after deletion, and why. Deletion is not total erasure
     everywhere. The following are kept because Win's VPN is required or
     entitled to keep them:
       Payment and donation records (PayPal, BTCPay, and Monero order records
         and payment webhook records, including the buyer email supplied at
         checkout) are retained for tax, accounting, and audit reasons. Win's
         VPN Inc. is a 501(c)(3) organisation that must file an annual
         Form 990-series return and substantiate contributions, and payment
         records are also needed to resolve chargebacks and disputes. This
         includes the written acknowledgment issued for a charitable
         contribution (the donor name, any donor email address, the amount, and
         the date or dates of the gift), because that document is the
         substantiation the IRS requires a charity to be able to produce. An
         acknowledgment belongs to the donor rather than to an account, so
         deleting an account does not remove it.
       Prepaid redemption records. The record that a particular prepaid code was
         redeemed is retained so the same code cannot be redeemed twice. The
         account number on that record is replaced by a one-way pseudonym. A
         separate append-only redemption audit log is protected against
         modification and deletion by a database-level control that exists
         specifically to stop tampering, so entries in that log, which can
         include the account number, are retained for the life of the log.
       Referral records. A referral links two accounts, and deleting one must
         not corrupt or silently orphan the other party's record. The referral
         record is kept, but the deleted account's number is replaced with a
         one-way pseudonym. The referrer keeps bonus days already granted: the
         payment that funded the referral still stands, and a third party
         should not lose service time because someone else exercised a privacy
         right. If that funding payment is later refunded, cancelled, or
         reversed, the bonus can still be reversed.
       Security and abuse-prevention records are keyed to hashed forms of an IP
         address, device identifier, or account number rather than to the values
         themselves, are held in a separate store for up to 180 days, and are
         retained for security, fraud prevention, and abuse response.
       Short-lived rate-limit counters are keyed to one-way hashes, contain no
         account number or address, and expire on their own.
       A deletion audit record is written so Win's VPN can demonstrate that the

§16  PLATFORM-SPECIFIC DISCLOSURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
16.1 Android VpnService. On Android, Win's VPN uses Android VpnService as a
     core user-facing VPN function. After the user starts a connection and
     approves Android's standard VPN permission prompt, VpnService creates an
     encrypted device-level tunnel to a Win's VPN endpoint, routes selected
     device traffic through that tunnel, supports user-selected split-tunneling
     exclusions, and shows the Android VPN indicator/notification while active.
     Win's VPN does not use VpnService to inject ads, monetize traffic,
     manipulate advertising, sell traffic data, or collect browsing history, DNS
     query history, traffic contents, or destination activity.
16.2 Android installed-app visibility for split tunneling. When an Android user
     opens Exclude Apps From VPN (Split Tunneling), Win's VPN first shows a
     separate installed-app access disclosure. The app accesses installed
     application names, icons, and package identifiers only after the user
     affirmatively chooses Continue; choosing Not now leaves the inventory
     untouched and the VPN remains usable without per-app exclusions. The
     installed-app inventory is used locally so the user can search for and
     select apps to exclude from the VPN. The full inventory is not uploaded to
     Win's VPN, shared with third parties, sold, or used for advertising or
     analytics. Only the package identifiers the user selects are retained in
     local app settings and passed locally to Android VpnService to apply those
     exclusions. The user can remove selections in the split-tunneling picker,
     or delete all locally retained selections by clearing Win's VPN app data or
     uninstalling the app.
16.3 Camouflage safety mode. Android and iOS launcher icon camouflage and
     Android/iOS in-app calculator camouflage are user-safety features for
     high-risk censorship environments, including Burma/Myanmar, where a
     visible VPN interface can expose users to retaliation. On Android and iOS
     both are applied automatically once a device is activated and cannot be
     switched off in the app; the real interface is shown after the PIN is
     entered. Android TV and desktop builds have neither. They do not hide malicious activity,
     iOS may display an operating-system confirmation when changing the icon,
     Android's VPN notification remains user-controlled, and the operating
     system's VPN status remains visible while a tunnel is active.
16.4 Trusted contact and duress response. Win's VPN does not ship this
     feature. There is no duress code, no duress wipe, and no trusted-contact
     alert on any platform, including Android and iOS. No duress credential can
     be enrolled, no trusted-contact phone number or email is requested or
     stored, the Android release build declares no SMS permission and sends no
     SMS, and the app transmits no contact, coordinate, or alert to Win's VPN
     servers or to any third party.
