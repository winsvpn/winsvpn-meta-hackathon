How a Charitable Deduction Actually Works

Read this first: most filers get no itemized deduction for a gift. A deduction reduces the income you are taxed on. It is not a credit, and it does not cut your tax bill dollar for dollar. Most people take the standard deduction, which for 2026 is $16,100 for a single filer, $32,200 for a married couple filing jointly, and $24,150 for a head of household. If everything you could itemize adds up to less than that, a charitable gift produces no itemized deduction for you at all.

One exception, new for 2026, and it is small. A filer who takes the standard deduction may still deduct up to $1,000 of cash contributions, or $2,000 on a joint return. That ceiling is the whole benefit. If you do itemize, 2026 also added a floor: only the part of your giving above 0.5% of adjusted gross income counts. See IRS Topic 506 and Publication 505.

Three illustrations. These state their assumptions; they are not predictions about you. A $100 gift from a single filer with roughly $60,000 of income who takes the standard deduction reduces taxable income by the full $100, worth about $12 less federal tax at a 12% marginal rate. A $10,000 gift from a couple with $300,000 of income who itemize loses $1,500 to the new floor, leaving $8,500 deductible, about $2,040 at 24%. A $100,000 gift from a single filer with $600,000 of income who itemizes loses $3,000 to the floor, leaving $97,000, about $33,950 at 35%. In every case the gift still costs what it costs.

Gifts of $250 or more need a written acknowledgment, and the deduction cannot be claimed without one. If you gave $250 or more to Win's VPN Inc. and did not receive one from us, email support@winsvpn.org and we will issue it. Separately, a deduction generally cannot exceed 60% of adjusted gross income; anything above the ceiling carries forward for up to five years. See Publication 526 and Publication 1771.

This is general information about the law, not tax advice. What a gift is worth to you depends on your filing status, your income, whether you itemize, and what else you gave. State treatment varies and does not follow the federal rules. Win's VPN Inc. cannot advise you on your return. Please talk to a tax professional and check the rules yourself at irs.gov. Please give because you want the work funded, not because you expect the government to hand the money back.

How Referrals Work

Paid accounts get a referral link inside the app. Free access is not tied to a personal account, so it cannot earn or grant referral time, though free users can still share the Win's VPN website. When someone buys a paid plan through your link and that payment settles, we add the same number of days to both accounts: 20% of the term that purchase covers, rounded up to whole days. A one-month plan works out to roughly 6 or 7 days each, a one-year plan to about 73 days each, and a 10-year lifetime pass to about 731 days each. The bonus is a set number of days taken from the purchase you referred, not a percentage of your own remaining time, so referring a one-month buyer adds about a week to your account whichever plan you are on.

Each referred account can earn this once. Later renewals by the same person do not pay again, you cannot refer yourself, and a code only counts while the referring account is still active; if it has lapsed, neither side gets the bonus. There is no limit on how many different people you can refer. The days are added as soon as the payment settles rather than after a waiting period. If that payment is later refunded, reversed, cancelled, suspended, or fails, the bonus days are taken back off the referring account and the referred account loses the access that payment funded.

If someone sent you a referral link, download Win's VPN, choose a plan, and complete the purchase through that link. The code travels with the payment and is applied to both accounts once the payment settles.
