Facts from the current app:
- The app is a VPN client.
- The app uses encryption for VPN tunneling and secure network communication.
- The app uses Apple's Network Extension Packet Tunnel framework.
- The primary tunnel protocol is Winn v3. Its packet engine uses an
  industry-standard, published suite: X25519 key agreement (RFC 7748), the
  NIST post-quantum key-encapsulation standard ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203), and
  the ChaCha20-Poly1305 AEAD (RFC 8439), with a SHA-256 transcript hash.
  The Apple Release builds also compile three active Ed25519 (RFC 8032)
  descriptor-verification public keys through the iOS and macOS Winn v3
  descriptor-ring xcconfig files. The Winn v3 protocol separately defines an
  optional Ed25519 plus
  post-quantum ML-DSA-65 (NIST FIPS 204) dual-signature attestation leg,
  but that leg is gated off in the Apple builds and is not active in the
  iOS or macOS app.
- The app includes local AWG/Xray packet-engine artifacts in Apple extension
  bundles during release validation. AWG (AmneziaWG) and Xray are the Winn v1
  compatibility path, used only when Winn v3 cannot connect, with AWG tried
  first and Xray second.
- The shipped protocol surface includes obfuscation and transport framing
  outside the published cryptographic primitives: AmneziaWG randomized junk
  and padding parameters, enabled Xray transports, Winn v3 handshake framing,
  and single-packet authorization. The owner-approved answer therefore treats
  proprietary or non-standard encryption as Yes pending counsel classification.
- The app uses standard platform security APIs such as Apple Keychain for local
  identity/key storage.
- The app is not a medical, military, or government-only app.
- The app is intended for general consumer/business privacy and network
  security use.
- There is no current 1.0.17+34 signed IPA or PKG. Unsigned, ad-hoc, Apple
  Development, placeholder, or historical 1.0.14+29 identities cannot carry an
  encryption declaration or export-compliance receipt.
- The owner-approved conservative submission answer is
  ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption=YES. A later signed IPA and PKG may carry that
  answer only after exact-artifact verification. They must not carry an
  ITSEncryptionExportComplianceCode until Apple issues one for accepted
  documentation.
- The signed-in App Information page currently has no approved encryption
  declaration or uploaded document.
- Apple TV is not a shipping surface for 1.0.17+34, so it has no export
  declaration or encryption receipt.

Current primary-source requirements:
- Apple requires an export-compliance determination whenever an app uses,
  accesses, contains, implements, or incorporates encryption.
- Apple says industry-standard encryption implemented outside the operating
  system requires a French encryption declaration when distributing in France.
- Apple says proprietary or non-standard encryption additionally requires a
  U.S. CCATS, as well as the French declaration for France.
- Apple directs developers to populate the app description and manage App
  Store availability before submitting required encryption documentation;
  without those records Apple cannot determine whether the documents are
  sufficient.
- Apple says complete encryption-document submissions are evaluated case by
  case and are expected to clear in approximately two business days.
- BIS places encryption software in Category 5, Part 2 and states that the
  applicable classification, reporting, end-user, end-use, and destination
  requirements must be determined before relying on License Exception ENC.
