# Round 40 addendum stage2 (turn 2): claude-opus5

**OI-1: DISAGREE.** The text ranks rate-dependent policing as the 2nd compounding cause of the 445 plateau, but the packet's own Mathis derivation forbids that at the operating point: 437 inner across 32 streams is 13.7 Mbps per flow, and inverting Mathis (p = (1.22 · MSS·8 / (BW·RTT))²) gives p = 0.11% at 30 ms RTT and 1.0% even at an implausibly short 10 ms RTT, so the tunnel's carrier loss at 470 offered is 5x to 50x below the 5.35-5.89% the open-loop probe took at a *lower* 400 offered. Both cannot describe the same path at the same time. Minimal amendment I will vote AGREE on: rank 1st the phone uplink delivery envelope; record policing as an unresolved ceiling *above* the current plateau, or as a probe-vantage artifact (fact B was sent from the LAN vantage, not from the handset radio), pending one measurement: per-tunnel carrier loss counters sampled at the 437-445 plateau. Under 0.5% there confirms policing does not bind at 470 and the phone radio is the sole first-rank cause; over 4% falsifies the Mathis inversion and my objection dies with it.

**OI-2: AGREE.** Fact B settles it at matched mean rate: paced 5.83 vs burst 5.35/5.89 at 400, paced 8.76/9.87 vs burst 8.16/8.28 at 550. Burst shape is not the loss term, so pacing cannot be a capacity lever, and gating it on queue-delay data is the correct disposition.

**OI-3: DISAGREE**, narrowly, on the product wording only. The technical core is sound and the arithmetic survives a tighter overhead model: at 4.3% carrier overhead (60 bytes on a 1400 inner payload) the requirement is 1043 rather than 1070, against 560-580 best measured delivery, so 1000 tunneled up is out of reach either way. But "saturates your uplink" is falsified on this exact vantage if the ISP uplink exceeds roughly 500 Mbps, since the tunnel delivers 470 and the packet itself attributes the deficit to the handset radio, not the uplink. The claim also cannot be certified today because ISP plan symmetry is still an open owner-report item in the same OI. Replace with a claim bounded by what is measured, for example "delivers your device's full tested uplink rate", and OI-3 gets my AGREE.

**OI-4: AGREE.** Fact D shows PMTUD is EMSGSIZE-driven with zero emitted probes, so no redesign is forced, and a forward rule of AEAD-sealed or CSPRNG-filled is the right floor given constant-pattern payloads die near 100% at any rate. Non-blocking note, not a condition on my vote: order the rule AEAD-first and treat CSPRNG-fill as the pre-key exception only, since an unauthenticated probe still emits something unbidden and sits outside the per-connection salt discipline in carrier/doc.go.

**OI-5: AGREE.** Pushing iperf3 over adb to a LAN receiver isolates radio and AP from WAN path and tunnel processing, and the 470-versus-550 discriminator is the right cut. Recommended instrumentation, not a condition: snapshot the TX retry counter before and after each sweep step so the 9.2% figure becomes a per-test delta, because a cumulative count over 2.6M lifetime packets is not a measurement of retry rate at the plateau, and the OI-1 first-rank claim leans on it. Concur that the toybox-nc run at ~130 Mbps is tool-limited and carries no evidentiary weight.

**Steelman, opus (my Turn 1, send-gate BDP):** the strongest form was that a token window applied at Seal time would impose a hard outstanding-bytes cap, making throughput × RTT constant and explaining a plateau that tracks no queue or radio signal, which would have been the cleanest single-cause account of a suspiciously flat 437-445. Fact A refutes it: params.go:164-177 scopes the window to receiver precompute, reorder tolerance, and replay filtering, and core/session.go:384-403 seals against sendPRF.token(counter) with no window check, no ACK, no credit, no outstanding cap. My own stated rank-1 kill condition fired. I withdraw send-gate BDP entirely and do not carry it forward in any form.

**Steelman, agy (burst-induced loss):** the strongest form was that 32-token microbursts arrive faster than a shallow AP or policer queue drains, so loss would be shape-dependent rather than rate-dependent and pacing would recover capacity at unchanged mean rate. Fact B tests exactly that and the effect is absent at both 400 and 550, with burst marginally *better* at 550. The mechanism is dead as a capacity argument. The part of agy's position that facts A to D strengthen is the Wi-Fi TX/RX asymmetry: TX PHY 1441 against RX PHY 1921 with 9.2% TX retries, which is why 1166 down proves only the Rx side and says nothing about the uplink.
